## Intended use

Estimate, for the simulator and the chatbot, whether the visible flame of a single burning fuel droplet goes out on its own in a given microgravity atmosphere, within the conditions FLEX tested. It supports engineering judgment and does not replace material certification.

## Support and confidence

- Space: pressure_atm, o2, co2, he, d0_mm, standardized, one index per `fuel`.
- Joint range threshold (percentile 95 of each training row's distance to its nearest other test): heptane: 0.7236, methanol: 0.8300.
- `in_tested_range` is true when the nearest test is within the threshold and no input is outside its own tested range.
- `support_n` counts training tests within the threshold; `nearest_tests` lists the 5 closest.
- `confidence`: low if out of range or support_n < 3; high if in range and support_n >= 5 and probability of the predicted class >= 0.7; otherwise medium.

## Limitations

- Microgravity only: gravity is not an input, and the prediction must not change with any gravity control.
- The class no_extinction joins tests that burned to completion and tests where the droplet broke apart or left its support fiber (disruption). A disruption early in the burn can hide an extinction that would have happened later (decision D-01).
- Extinction means extinction of the visible flame. Heptane droplets can keep burning as a dim cool flame after the visible flame goes out.
- Pressure was tested at two levels only (see the coverage facts). The per-input limits accept any pressure between the two levels, and the joint range check only rejects pressures far from both of them.
- Suppressants (CO2, He) were tested only at moderate oxygen levels. The Moon-like region has tests without suppressant only.
- Fiber-supported tests are used for training, but every prediction is made for a free droplet.
- Tests above the pressure limit are excluded: there, pressure is confounded with a single CO2 mixture.
- The slow imposed flow used in some tests (droplet translation) is not an input.
